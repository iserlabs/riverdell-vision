The verdict in one line

This is a genuinely well-built site with a real design language of its own, and it is losing to itself in four places: a form that can drop a patient enquiry without telling anyone, a hero that fills the window at no screen size, a display typeface running with its best feature switched off, and the two pages that answer "what does it cost" reachable from nowhere.

hero · type · colour · components · motion

Where the biggest visible gap sits.

The hero fills the window at no screen size

There is no viewport-height rule anywhere in the marketing site. Measured across seven geometries on four routes, 26 of 28 checks fail. The homepage overshoots the fold by 531 pixels on an iPhone and falls 200 pixels short at 1920 wide. On a landscape phone the first section runs to 373% of the window.

The right-hand panel on the homepage is a mocked-up calendar with dummy date cells and no real availability behind it. That same text-left, card-right construction repeats across eleven service pages with only the image swapped. It is the single loudest template tell on the site.

Newsreader is running with its best feature switched off

The font loader never requests the optical-sizing axis, so Google serves the 16-point master and pins it. A 64-pixel heading is therefore a text-grade letterform scaled up: hairlines too thin, spacing too loose. Proven by measurement, a 64px string renders identically at four different optical sizes. One line in the layout fixes every heading on the site.

src/app/layout.tsx:9 · needs axes: ["opsz"]

The site already owns a better design language than it uses

The numbered hairline rows, the ghost-numeral proof ledger, the self-drawing process timeline and the lens-ring studio panel are genuinely good, hand-built work. Then 67 containers across the marketing pages fall back to the same bordered card, 56 of them with no shadow, no fill change and no ring. Card sits 1.07:1 above the page background, a three percent step, which is precisely why every card needs a border to exist at all. That number is the flat-templated read in one measurement.

Motion is careful and says nothing

83 identical fade-ups, one considered easing curve, and no signature moment. Two measured defects: the magnetic effect on the primary Book button computes its pull from its own moved position, so it visibly wobbles for 600 milliseconds under a stationary cursor and runs up to 40 pixels away from where you aimed. And the global smooth-scroll applies to page navigation, so every internal link costs about a second of animated scrolling and lands you 93 pixels down the new page.

src/components/site/magnetic.tsx:28 · src/app/globals.css:133

The palette brief and the shipped palette are two different briefs

The stylesheet comment says warm-luxe, cream canvas. The values are cool blue at hue 240 to 254. Warmth survives only in the coral and brass accents. Defensible for a medical practice, but it is worth deciding on purpose rather than by drift.

Tier 3 · Business

conversion · copy · architecture

Measured on a cold phone: a call is one tap, a Zocdoc booking is one scroll and one tap, the request form is five taps and one page load. Those are good numbers. Three things undercut them.

  1. HighTwo dominant actions compete in one viewport. "Request an appointment" and "See available times" are both solid teal, go to different destinations, and the Zocdoc one is physically larger.
  2. HighThe pages answering the top two objections are unreachable by clicking. Cost and insurance is the number one question a parent has, and the page that answers it is in no menu.
  3. MediumThe form requires an email to promise a phone call. Name and phone is the honest minimum.
  4. MediumThe final ask on every page carries no proof beside it. Three buttons, no rating, no credential, no review.
  5. MediumAn investor pitch and a staff dashboard are linked in the patient footer. A patient landing on lender projections reads as a practice being sold. The data room passphrase is hardcoded in the browser bundle, so treat that page as fully public. src/components/site/footer.tsx:162 · src/components/site/expansion-gate.tsx:10

The copy claims care instead of showing the facts it already has

The clinical writing is strong: every service and condition page opens with a direct, checkable answer and refuses to over-promise. The connective tissue is where it sounds generated. Twelve headlines fail the transfer test, meaning you could change two nouns and move the sentence to any dental practice in America. The homepage hero lead is one of them.

Meanwhile the genuinely differentiating facts sit unused underneath: a Fellow of the College of Optometrists in Vision Development on staff, five specialty programmes run in-house that most practices refer out, four named optometrists, a real 5.0 across 448 Google reviews, patients who have stayed thirty years. The words "real", "genuine", "honest" and "truly" appear 17 times in the area copy alone. Candour is behaviour, not a word you add.

The five biggest levers

Ranked by what they change for a parent deciding in two minutes.

  1. Make a lost lead loud, and give the hero a real window. The first is worth every enquiry the form has ever taken. The second removes the loudest template tell and puts the practice's actual room on screen in the first second.
  2. Turn on optical sizing and give the light surfaces a real step. Two small changes that lift every heading and every card across all 24 routes without redesigning anything.
  3. Put cost and common questions in the navigation, and relabel Care by problem. One file. Converts two stranded pages into the site's second and third most useful.
  4. Rewrite the answers so each one names the practice and the town in its first sentence, then push those same sentences into the file assistants actually read. This is what decides whether an AI recommends Riverdell by name.
  5. Swap claimed care for the credential in the three highest-traffic sentences. The Fellow, the five in-house programmes, the four named doctors and the 448 reviews are all already in the repository, sitting underneath sentences that describe them in adjectives.

What is already good, and should not be touched

The proof ledger, the editorial index rows, the process timeline and the studio panel are real design work. The review system is honest and verifiable end to end. The redirect map, security headers and structured data are better than most agency output. The reduced-motion and no-JavaScript behaviour is correct by construction, not by accident. None of that needs replacing.
