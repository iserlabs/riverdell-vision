# The response test

Ten minutes of your hands. It is the one input this design pass cannot get by reading the
repo, and it is the largest measured effect in the whole field.

## Why it exists

Between a five-minute and a thirty-minute reply, the odds of qualifying a lead fall about
21x. Across an audit of 2,241 companies the average first response was 42 hours and 23%
never replied at all. And operators do not know their own number: in home services 54% of
call centres claimed sub-five-minute replies while platform data across 132,188 campaigns
showed 88% taking longer, the most common answer being a full day. **Roughly 15x
self-overstatement**, which is why asking Dr. Han produces a figure the design cannot rest on.

A form is a promise about a human. If nobody answers it inside the hour, no amount of
typography recovers the loss, and the honest move is to design toward the channel that does
get answered.

## Why I could not run it myself

1) The live request form at riverdellvision.com is still the Roya site, not our build, so
testing our form would measure our own inbox rather than Riverdell's front desk.
2) That live form is behind reCAPTCHA v3. Scripting a submission means defeating a bot
check, which I am not going to do.
3) It requires a phone number, and I do not have one for you.

## The three timings worth having

### 1) The form

1) Open https://www.riverdellvision.com/request-an-appointment.html
2) Fill Name, Phone Number, Email with your own real details.
3) In the Message box write: Website test from Jake, no appointment needed, just checking how
   the request form comes through. Sorry for the noise.
4) Press Submit and note the exact time.
5) Note the exact time of the first human reply, by any channel, and which channel it came on.

### 2) The phone

1) Call (201) 265-7900 during working hours and time how long until a person picks up.
2) Call again on a Saturday and note what happens: a person, a machine, or nothing.

Where calls dominate a category, 42% of phone leads convert during the call itself. Whether
this practice is a phone business or a Zocdoc business is a per-client fact, not an
assumption, so this number decides how much of the page the phone affordance earns.

### 3) Zocdoc

Book a real slot on Zocdoc and see what arrives, then cancel it. Zocdoc is locked as the
single primary action everywhere, so if it confirms instantly and the form takes a day, that
is not a flaw, it is the argument for the lock. Worth proving rather than assuming.

## What each answer changes

**Under an hour on the form.** The form earns equal weight with Zocdoc, and the page can
carry a stated response time, which is a strong trust signal.

**Several hours to a day.** The form becomes an explicit fallback, worded so nobody expects
a fast reply, and the phone and Zocdoc carry the page.

**Nobody answers weekends.** The out-of-hours affordance points at Zocdoc, never at a call
button. A click-to-call button pointed at an unanswered phone is a negative-value feature.

Whatever the number is, **never print a response promise the practice has not agreed to
keep.** An unkept "we reply within the hour" is worse than silence, because it converts a
slow reply into a broken promise.
